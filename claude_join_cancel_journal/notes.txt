what do you mean by 'replay of orderbook removals for late arrivals'?

“The replay” is what on_place_late does when the host tells it late that an order is ours. It doesn’t re-apply anything to the book. It reads back the tactic’s own journal of recent book updates and works out the state the order would have had if on_place had been called at its Add.

Why it’s needed. Between the order’s Add and the late call, the tactic treated the order as a market order, so it kept no state for it:

(b) never saw which round lots were ahead of it, or which of them later cancelled.
(d) never counted the executions above its price.
(c) never took the NBBO at its Add as its baseline.

What makes it possible. Every book update of a tracked symbol writes a 32-byte journal entry: the event, side, price, the queue handle of the order it touched, that order’s size before and after, its flags and the timestamp. NBBO changes and (b) cancels of a whole price level get entries too.

What the replay does at the late call:

It walks back through that symbol’s entries until it finds the order’s own Add, matched by its queue handle.
It goes forward over the updates at the order’s price since the Add. Orders added there after ours are behind it. Each order that was ahead and left counts as either a cancellation or a fill, and its size at our Add says whether it held a round lot.
From that it rebuilds the counts each trigger needs. For (b): round lots ahead at the Add and how many of them cancelled. For (d): executions above its price. For (c): the NBBO at the Add.
It finds the first entry where a trigger would have fired, if any, and makes that decision now. It also keeps the counts exactly as they stood at that decision.

Example at 99.00. Round lots R1 and R2 are resting; our order L arrives unrecognised; R1 cancels; R2 cancels; then the host learns L is ours.

The replay finds L’s Add and sees two round lots ahead of it, both gone by cancellation. After R2’s removal L was at the front with 2 of 2 cancelled, so (b) held at that entry. L is cancelled at the call, together with any other order of ours at 99.00.

What “the replay gives up” meant in the review. If an update at the order’s price arrives without the touched order’s handle, the replay can’t tell whether that order was ahead or behind ours. It then stops, and the order is tracked from the call as on_place would. A hidden execution at that price is one such update, which is finding 3.
